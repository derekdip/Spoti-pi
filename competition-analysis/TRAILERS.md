## Per-trailer notes

**Game Night.**
- **First 2 s:** mixed-reality capture of two real people on a couch, wearing headsets and playing around a coffee table. The logo appears at 0:02.
- **Body:** a narrated voiceover explains why they made tabletop mode. All seven minigames are shown, almost all with both players visibly playing.
- **Verdict:** the clearest social proof in the field. It shows people having fun together, not just a mechanic.

**Bots & Pans.**
- **Opening:** gameplay from 0:00, a mixed-reality restaurant on a real table.
- **Multiplayer section:** labeled "Next Up: Multiplayer Gameplay", with robot avatars and unscripted voice chat ("Can you help?", laughter, "let's high five").
- **Verdict:** raw but authentic, the best evidence of fun after Game Night. There is no voiceover explaining the premise.

**23:17.**
- **Opening:** portal visuals, with "Same desk. Two eras." on screen by 0:04.
- **Real players:** at 0:18–0:28 it shows two real people in separate rooms ("Two rooms. Connected by voice.").
- **Body:** handled puzzles, then a fire climax.
- **Verdict:** atmospheric and credible, though slow in the middle.

**Crokinole.**
- **Opening:** hands flicking discs from 0:00, with an elegant voiceover.
- **Features shown:** a four-way split-screen of the input modes; a mixed-reality board on a real table; online play with avatars; "Also on Windows PC and Android".
- **Verdict:** polished and calm. Social play is shown only briefly.

**Campy.**
- **Opening:** the first frame is a marshmallow avatar with a name tag (a social cue). The fire visuals are rich.
- **Body:** the captioned story (hospital campers, smoke allergy) does a lot of the work. Multiplayer (four campers, voice) appears late, at about 2:00.
- **Verdict:** long fire-play stretches confirm "lots of atmosphere, little to do".

**Kaiju Corp.**
- **Opening:** the first 0–10 s are 2D anime cinematics, not gameplay.
- **Body:** a real person in a Quest, gesture labels (Finger Gun, Archery, Shield, Voice-control Laser Breath), and "Mobile Integration" with phone gameplay at 1:18. It ends with footage from public showcase events.
- **Edit:** rough, including black frames at 0:52–0:54.
- **Verdict:** more credible than its screenshots suggested. It confirms an Acron-like VR-versus-PC/mobile format.

**RULE MAKER.**
- **Style:** a cinematic, moody thriller edit carried by text cards ("Two cards. Their total is your score", "Here, you make the rules").
- **Gameplay:** the voice-AI rule creation appears at about 1:04.
- **Verdict:** stylish, but the rules are hard to follow at trailer speed. The rival is only a score and is never shown as a person.

**Let's Flock!**
- **Structure:** a narrated feature tour (no heads-up display, magpie kill feed, Most Wanted board, modes, wardrobe, achievements).
- **Players shown:** mostly bots. No visible group of friends.
- **Verdict:** competent dev-log style, but repetitive, and it ends on menus.

**Sneak-A-Snack.**
- **Opening:** about 28 s of slow narrative (cave, fire, tent) before the title.
- **Menus:** 0:44–1:02 is camp-code menu entry.
- **Players:** the two players are never shown simultaneously, so the core moment of one person reading another's shadow never lands visually.

**Doodle Figure.**
- **0:00–0:04.5:** a static title card ("they draw — you sculpt it blind") on black.
- **0:04–0:12:** curtain, room code, and a start panel reading "4 bots + 1 phone seated".
- **0:14–0:20:** phone screen recordings of joining and drawing ("phones join in the browser — no app").
- **0:22–0:48 (the best section):** split screen of the phone drawing a lion next to the VR sculpting, captioned "friends doodle the secret word" / "you sculpt what you think it is". This explains the asymmetric loop clearly, and the lion sculpture reads reasonably well.
- **0:50–1:10:** about 20 s on the "IT WAS LION" reveal and the crowd. Too long, and the gesture interactions (wave, bow, arms up) are not clearly called out.
- **1:12–1:14:** the keepsake on the phone.
- **1:16–1:18:** "MONKEY" and "DONUT" reveals without their sculptures.
- **1:20–1:27:** end card.
- **Audio:** the only spoken lines are "And the word was…" and "The crowd goes wild." No voiceover.
- **Missing:** real people, several phones drawing at once, laughter, a headset being passed around.
- **Length:** at 87 s it is the shortest, so about half the allowed time is unused.

## Re-cut plan (store page and future competitions; the submission itself is locked)

1. **0–3 s:** real people on a couch, phones up, laughing, while someone in a headset sculpts. Film it on a phone; you need people on camera, not a mixed-reality capture rig. Put the title over this footage instead of on black.
2. **3–10 s:** one punchy line: "One headset. Everyone else just uses their phone."
3. **10–40 s:** three phones drawing at the same time (a quick grid), cut against time-lapsed sculpting. Keep the split screen; it works.
4. **40–60 s:** the reveal at about 6 s, not 20 s, plus one clear shot of the crowd responding to a wave or bow, labeled as such.
5. **60–90 s:** 3–4 rapid rounds, each showing the sculpture next to its answer. Funny misses sell a party game better than successes.
6. **90–110 s:** the headset handed to the next person with the room still running; the keepsake landing on everyone's phone with real reactions.
7. **End card:** doodlefigure.com, "no app · bots for solo · hands only".

## Notes

- **Hand Riders:** close to commercial quality. Many distinct worlds (desert, rapids, neon city, mines), a diorama lobby with characters to pick, and fast cutting. Social presence is mostly AI racers.
- **PullFit:** a real person training with bands outdoors in mixed reality. Its multiplayer section shows three real people doing pass-and-play and co-location, ending in confetti. Strong social proof.
- **Desk Session:** a mixed-reality desk turned skatepark, physics fingerboarding and a park builder. Same-room multiplayer appears only briefly.
- **Boplo:** clean captions ("Mess with your friend", "Sabotage encouraged") over an online duel against an avatar opponent.
- **Donkey:** mixed reality on a real table with robot avatars, and a real person shown playing. Clear rules captions. Bots fill empty seats.
- **Lumenoir:** a sleek co-watching pitch ("Two homes. One film. Same second."). Other viewers appear as glowing orbs. Co-watching is named in the award text.
- **Snappy Planet:** short and punchy with "8 players online", but other players are seen only briefly.
- **In Your Hands:** a polished game show with varied gesture rounds. The duel appears briefly.
- **Bite or Betray, Gesture Royale:** clear, but other players are mostly bots.
- **Project Arena:** a real person outdoors playing against a bot. Clean, but no social footage.
- **Table Hub:** heavy on setup (measuring hand size, fitting the table). Looks like Liar's Bar.
- **Moonrise:** a solo tabletop golf and pinball showcase. Multiplayer is not shown.
- **Mothlight:** dark and abstract. The social-deduction rules are explained, but no people appear.
- **Watch Together:** a raw walkthrough ("no one's here right now").
- **Toy Takeover:** about 7 minutes long, so judges stop at 3:00, and the first 4 minutes are top-down grey test footage.
