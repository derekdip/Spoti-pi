## Executable Coordinate Systems: current theoretical framework, empirical results, and open frontier (preserved text)

### 1. Central problem

Many high-dimensional states are expensive to store, transmit, fit, or reproduce directly. Standard compression seeks a lower-dimensional representation such as

$$
x\approx Uz,
\qquad
x\in\mathbb R^N,
\quad
z\in\mathbb R^n.
$$

Executable Coordinate Systems (ECS) asks a different question:

Can the state be represented more economically by a small collection of parameters that cause a shared executable mechanism to regenerate it?

The basic representation is

$$
\boxed{x\approx G(\theta)}
$$

where G is executable code and

$$
\theta\in\mathbb R^k,
\qquad
k\ll N.
$$

A hybrid representation adds a small residual model:

$$
\boxed{x\approx G(\theta)+Uz.}
$$

More generally,

$$
\boxed{x\approx G(\theta)+U(\theta)z,}
$$

where the residual frame itself moves with the executable state.

The central distinction is therefore not simply linear versus nonlinear compression. It is between storing outcomes and storing compact causes together with a reusable decoder.

---

### 2. Executable generative dimension versus linear dimension

Suppose

$$
\mathcal X_M=\{x_1,\ldots,x_M\}
$$

is a family of normalized states.

Define the worst-case rank-n linear approximation error

$$
e_n^{\mathrm{lin}}(\mathcal X_M)
=
\inf_{\dim U=n}
\max_i
\|(I-P_U)x_i\|.
$$

If the states are generated by

$$
x_i=G(\theta_i),
\qquad
\theta_i\in\mathbb R^k,
$$

then the executable dynamic dimension is k, independently of the dimension of

$$
\operatorname{span}\{x_1,\ldots,x_M\}.
$$

This allows a fundamental separation:

$$
\boxed{
\text{small generative dimension}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{small global linear dimension}.
}
$$

---

### 3. Orthogonal separation theorem

Suppose

$$
x_1,\ldots,x_M
$$

are orthonormal.

For any rank-n orthogonal projector P,

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|Px_i\|^2\le n.
$$

Therefore some i satisfies

$$
\|Px_i\|^2\le\frac nM.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
e_n^{\mathrm{lin}}(\mathcal X_M)^2
\ge
1-\frac nM.
}
$$

To guarantee error at most $\varepsilon$,

$$
\boxed{
n\ge M(1-\varepsilon^2).
}
$$

If the same states are generated exactly by a fixed k-parameter executable family, then ECS uses k dynamic coordinates with zero error.

For fixed k,

$$
\frac{n_{\mathrm{linear}}}{k}
\rightarrow\infty
$$

$$
as M\rightarrow\infty.
$$

Thus executable coordinates can be arbitrarily more dimension-efficient than any global linear representation on suitable state families.

---

### 4. Low-coherence extension

Exact orthogonality is unnecessary.

Define

$\mu$
=
$\max_{i\ne$ j}
$|\langle$ $x_i,x_j\rangle|$.

Using the Gram matrix and Gershgorin's theorem,

$$
\lambda_{\max}
\le
1+(M-1)\mu.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
e_n^{\mathrm{lin}}(\mathcal X_M)^2
\ge
1-
\frac{
n[1+(M-1)\mu]
}{
M
}.
}
$$

Therefore achieving error at most $\varepsilon$ requires

$$
\boxed{
n
\ge
\frac{
M(1-\varepsilon^2)
}{
1+(M-1)\mu
}.
}
$$

This establishes robustness of the separation under approximate rather than exact orthogonality.

---

### 5. Localized transformation families

The most important example is a localized template translated through space:

$$
G(c)(x)=f(x-c).
$$

Only the position c changes.

Thus executable dynamic dimension is one in 1D, two for planar translation, and d in d spatial dimensions.

If sufficiently separated placements of f are approximately orthogonal, then a large number M of possible positions creates a correspondingly large global linear approximation requirement.

For compactly supported responses of characteristic width w in a domain of size L,

$$
M\sim\frac{L}{w}
$$

in one dimension and approximately

$$
M\sim
\left(\frac{L}{w}\right)^d
$$

in d dimensions.

Hence global linear dimension can scale approximately as

$$
\boxed{
n_{\mathrm{linear}}
=
\Omega\left(
\left(\frac{L}{w}\right)^d
\right)
}
$$

while executable location dimension remains d.

---

### 6. Hybrid executable representations

Many real states are not exactly generated by one mechanism.

Consider

$$
\boxed{
x=G(\theta)+Uz,
}
$$

where G describes large structured variation and Uz captures small residual variation.

If the executable orbit requires k coordinates and the residual has rank q, the hybrid uses approximately

$$
\boxed{k+q}
$$

dynamic coordinates.

A global linear representation must instead span both the nonlinear orbit and residual variation.

For M mutually orthogonal executable states and a q-dimensional orthogonal residual space, exact global linear representation requires

$$
\boxed{M+q}
$$

dimensions, while the executable hybrid requires only

$$
\boxed{k+q}.
$$

The residual dimension appears in both. The advantage comes from replacing the expensive global linear span of the orbit by its low-dimensional generative coordinates.

---

### 7. Moving residual frames

A stronger model is

$$
\boxed{
x=G(\theta)+U(\theta)z.
}
$$

This can be interpreted geometrically as a low-dimensional residual fiber attached to each point of the executable manifold.

The coordinates

$$
(\theta,z)
$$

have distinct meanings:

$$
\theta
=
\text{position on the executable base},
$$

$$
z
=
\text{local deviation around that state}.
$$

If the residual frame transforms with the mechanism, a global PCA representation may have to span many transformed copies of the same local residual directions.

Thus a q-dimensional moving residual frame can itself have very large global linear span.

---

### 8. Block-coherence theorem

Let each possible executable state have an orthonormal local frame

$$
B_i
=
[g_i,u_{i,1},\ldots,u_{i,q}]
\in\mathbb R^{N\times s},
\qquad
s=q+1.
$$

Define block coherence

$$
\mu_B
=
\max_{i\ne j}
\|B_i^\top B_j\|_2.
$$

Then a rank-n global linear representation satisfies

$$
\boxed{
\max_i
\frac{
\|(I-P)B_i\|_F^2
}{
s
}
\ge
1-
\frac{
n[1+(M-1)\mu_B]
}{
Ms
}.
}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
n
\ge
\frac{
Ms(1-\varepsilon^2)
}{
1+(M-1)\mu_B
}
}
$$

is necessary for normalized block error at most $\varepsilon^2$.

---

### 9. Local-overlap improvement

Worst-case coherence can be unnecessarily pessimistic for spatially local systems.

Define

$$
\rho_B
=
\max_i
\sum_{j\ne i}
\|B_i^\top B_j\|_2.
$$

Then

$$
\lambda_{\max}(B^\top B)
\le
1+\rho_B,
$$

giving

$$
\boxed{
n
\ge
\frac{
Ms(1-\varepsilon^2)
}{
1+\rho_B
}.
}
$$

If every local state overlaps substantially with at most d neighbors and each overlap is bounded by $\mu$,

$$
\rho_B\le d\mu,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{
n
\ge
\frac{
Ms(1-\varepsilon^2)
}{
1+d\mu
}.
}
$$

$$
For fixed d,\mu,s,
$$

$$
\boxed{n=\Omega(M).}
$$

This gives a broad class of systems where executable representations have an asymptotic advantage:

$$
\boxed{
\text{locally supported or summably interacting transformation families}.
}
$$

---

### 10. Continuous orbit formulation

Let

$$
G:\Theta\to\mathcal H,
\qquad
\Theta\subset\mathbb R^k,
$$

define a continuous executable orbit

$$
\mathcal M=G(\Theta).
$$

The global linear approximation problem is described by the Kolmogorov width

$$
d_n(\mathcal M)
=
\inf_{\dim U=n}
\sup_{x\in\mathcal M}
\|(I-P_U)x\|.
$$

An executable analogue is

$$
d_k^{\mathrm{exec}}(\mathcal M;\mathcal G)
=
\inf_{G\in\mathcal G_k}
\sup_{x\in\mathcal M}
\inf_\theta
\|x-G(\theta)\|.
$$

If the true orbit belongs to the executable grammar,

$$
\boxed{
d_k^{\mathrm{exec}}=0,
}
$$

while the linear width can remain large for many n.

This provides a natural approximation-theoretic interpretation of ECS.

---

### 11. Exact spectral example: random translation

Let

$$
X_c(t)=f(t-c),
\qquad
c\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(\mathbb T).
$$

Writing

$$
f(t)=
\sum_n
\hat f(n)e^{2\pi int},
$$

uniform translation yields covariance eigenfunctions

$$
\boxed{
\phi_n(t)=e^{2\pi int}
}
$$

with PCA eigenvalues

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_n=|\hat f(n)|^2.
}
$$

Thus PCA complexity of transported data is governed by the spectral richness of the transported template.

Executable complexity is governed instead by the dimension of the transformation.

This is a fundamental distinction.

---

### 12. Translated Gaussian theorem

For an $L^2$-normalized periodized Gaussian of width $\sigma$,

$$
\lambda_n
\propto
e^{-4\pi^2\sigma^2n^2}.
$$

The PCA rank required for RMS error $\varepsilon$ scales as

$$
\boxed{
r_{\mathrm{PCA}}
=
\Theta\left(
\sigma^{-1}
\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right)
}
$$

in one dimension.

In d dimensions,

$$
\boxed{
r_{\mathrm{PCA}}
=
\Theta\left[
\sigma^{-d}
\left(
\log\frac1\varepsilon
\right)^{d/2}
\right].
}
$$

The executable translation representation requires only

$$
\boxed{d}
$$

dynamic coordinates.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\frac{r_{\mathrm{PCA}}}{r_{\mathrm{exec}}}
\rightarrow\infty
}
$$

as localization becomes increasingly sharp,

$$
\sigma\rightarrow0.
$$

This provides an explicit continuous example of arbitrarily increasing linear/executable separation.

---

### 13. Template regularity controls linear difficulty

For transported states, PCA rank depends on spectral decay of the template.

Smooth analytic templates have rapidly decaying spectra.

Finite-smoothness templates produce polynomial decay.

Sharp or discontinuous templates have much slower spectral decay.

For a translated step-like function,

$$
|\hat f_n|
\sim\frac1{|n|}
$$

and therefore

$$
\lambda_n\sim\frac1{n^2}.
$$

The PCA tail scales approximately as

$$
\frac1m,
$$

so obtaining squared error $\varepsilon^2$ requires

$$
\boxed{
m=\Theta(\varepsilon^{-2}).
}
$$

The executable translation dimension remains one.

Thus sharp transported structures are particularly favorable candidates for executable representation.

---

### 14. Representation versus coding

A sparse dictionary can sometimes match the low per-state cost of executable coordinates.

For M possible localized states, an explicit dictionary can encode the active state using approximately

$$
O(\log M)
$$

bits.

But it must store the dictionary itself.

A rough structural comparison is:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{representation}
&
\text{shared model}
&
\text{dynamic state}
\\
\hline
\text{dense global linear}
&
O(NM)
&
O(M)
\\
\text{sparse dictionary}
&
O(NM)
&
O(\log M)
\\
\text{executable orbit}
&
O(N)\text{ or analytic rule}
&
O(\log M)
\end{array}
$$

Thus ECS can be interpreted as compressing the dictionary itself.

Instead of storing every transformed atom,

$$
d_1,\ldots,d_M,
$$

store a shared generator

$$
d_i=G(\theta_i).
$$

---

### 15. Executable rate scaling

Suppose G is K-Lipschitz over a bounded k-dimensional parameter domain.

Quantizing parameter space finely enough for state error $\varepsilon$ requires approximately

$$
\boxed{
R_{\mathrm{exec}}(\varepsilon)
=
O\left(
k\log\frac1\varepsilon
\right)
}
$$

bits per state, excluding shared decoder cost.

This should not be interpreted as a universal Shannon rate-distortion superiority result.

Rather, it shows that once an executable mechanism is shared, the state-description cost is controlled primarily by its generative dimension.

---

### 16. Deployment frontier

ECS should not optimize reconstruction error alone.

For representation R, measure

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C(R)
=
(
D,
B_s,
B_m,
C_e,
C_d
),
}
$$

where

- D: distortion;
- $B_s$: dynamic state bytes;
- $B_m$: shared model bytes;
- $C_e$: encoding cost;
- $C_d$: decoding/execution cost.

The real objective is the Pareto frontier over these resources.

An executable representation may tolerate slightly larger reconstruction error while substantially reducing state size, model size, or deployment cost.

This is precisely what appeared in the real-data experiments.

---

### 17. ECS-Search

Given a fixed executable grammar, ECS-Search compares executable candidates against generic fallbacks.

Conceptually,

$$
\boxed{
\min_R
D(R)+
\lambda_sB_s(R)+
\lambda_mB_m(R)+
\lambda_eC_e(R)+
\lambda_dC_d(R).
}
$$

The fallback is essential.

If the executable grammar is inappropriate, PCA or another generic representation should win.

This occurred repeatedly in the experiments and prevents ECS from becoming a mechanism-selection procedure that always declares success.

---

### 18. ECS-Discover

When the existing grammar fails, ECS-Discover analyzes the residual.

Let

$$
x\approx G(\theta)
$$

and residual

$$
r=x-G(\theta).
$$

The tangent space of the current executable model is

$$
T_\theta
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
\frac{\partial G}{\partial\theta_1},
\ldots,
\frac{\partial G}{\partial\theta_k}
\right\}.
$$

Decompose

$$
r=r_T+r_\perp.
$$

The tangential component

$$
r_T=P_Tr
$$

measures error removable by refining existing parameters.

The normal component

$$
r_\perp=(I-P_T)r
$$

contains evidence for genuinely missing structure.

This gives the first decision:

$$
\boxed{
\text{refine existing coordinates before expanding the grammar if residual energy is predominantly tangential}.
}
$$

---

### 19. Tangential improvement certificate

Define

$$
\boxed{
\tau
=
\frac{\|P_Tr\|^2}{\|r\|^2}.
}
$$

Large $\tau$ indicates substantial first-order removable error.

This prediction was tested retrospectively on the frozen ECG experiments.

At the grid-selected pulse parameters:

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{ECG200}}\approx0.389,
$$

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{ECGFiveDays}}\approx0.510.
$$

After continuous parameter refinement:

$$
\boxed{
\tau\approx0.004
}
$$

on both datasets.

Thus continuous refinement consumed essentially all tangent-space residual energy.

The certificate correctly identified that the encoder, not the grammar, was initially exhausted.

This is one of the strongest empirical validations of the geometric ECS framework.

---

### 20. Candidate extension scoring

After removing tangent-space error, candidate extension atom a should be projected into the current normal space:

$$
a_\perp=(I-P_T)a.
$$

Its first-order usefulness is measured by

$\boxed{$
S(a)
=
$\frac{$
$|\langle$ $r_\perp,a_\perp\rangle|^2$
}{
$\|a_\perp\|^2$
}.
}

This estimates how much residual energy the new direction can remove before performing a full nonlinear refit.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
\text{geometry proposes which direction is missing before committing to a new syntax}.
}
$$

---

### 21. Direction before syntax

Two candidate mechanisms can have different symbolic forms while producing nearly identical local extension directions.

Therefore candidate families should be compared geometrically before both are promoted.

Useful quantities include:

- mutual coherence;
- principal angles;
- projector distance;
- directed containment;
- winning-atom alignment.

The rule is:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{direction before syntax; syntax before promotion}.
}
$$

If two syntaxes produce essentially the same local direction, choose using reuse, MDL, or deployment cost rather than claiming two distinct discovered mechanisms.

---

### 22. Real-data equivalence-class result

This was tested on the frozen ECG residuals.

For ECG200, Gaussian-4 and derivative-of-Gaussian candidates initially scored almost identically.

Geometric comparison showed:

$$
\text{winning-atom alignment}\approx0.923,
$$

dominant-direction coherence

$$
\mu\approx0.995,
$$

and projector distance approximately

0.097.

Thus they were effectively one local extension direction wearing two syntaxes.

The correct conclusion was:

$$
\boxed{
\text{localized extension strongly supported; primitive identity unresolved}.
}
$$

For ECGFiveDays, however, the dominant directions were substantially different.

Thus extension equivalence was shown empirically to be state-dependent.

---

### 23. Frozen empirical sequence

The real-data program followed a strict sequence.

v1

A frozen generic grammar was compared against PCA fallbacks.

PCA-16 won all initial datasets.

v2

Residual evidence suggested generic localized pulses.

Only generic Gaussian pulse mechanisms and their sums were added.

The executable gap substantially narrowed on ECG datasets while the negative control remained unchanged.

v3

Residual geometry indicated parameter-grid quantization error.

Continuous refinement of existing pulse parameters was added.

The ECG200 executable hybrid reached essentially a dead heat with PCA-16 while using substantially less model/state storage.

Again the negative control remained fallback-dominated.

Transfer experiment

The untouched v3 runner was frozen before examining three unrelated physical datasets.

On MoteStrain,

$$
\boxed{
\text{pulses3r+pcaResidual2}
}
$$

became the first outright executable-class winner under the frozen criterion.

It also beat PCA-16 in raw normalized reconstruction error, not merely deployment score.

Earthquakes selected an executable model but all candidates reconstructed poorly, so that result remains weak evidence.

SonyAIBORobotSurface1 correctly selected PCA.

Thus the executable grammar transferred beyond ECG rather than merely encoding ECG-specific morphology.

---

### 24. A falsified prediction

Residual descriptors initially suggested that the next ECG primitive should be an odd derivative-of-Gaussian kernel.

This prediction was frozen before evaluation.

The geometric candidate scoring refuted it:

a fourth ordinary Gaussian scored slightly better.

Subsequent equivalence analysis showed that on ECG200 the two candidates represented almost the same missing direction.

This is important scientifically because ECS contains a documented failure of its own heuristic layer.

The geometry layer corrected the heuristic instead of the grammar being retroactively tuned.

### 25. Discovery protocol

The mature ECS loop is therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{fit current executable model}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{compute residual}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{project onto tangent space}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{refine if tangent energy remains}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{analyze normal residual}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{propose generic candidate extensions}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{score candidate directions}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{collapse geometrically equivalent candidates}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{freeze prediction}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{evaluate on untouched data}\\
&\downarrow\\
&\text{promote only if deployment/generalization evidence supports it}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

This separates:

$$
\boxed{
\text{ECS-Search}
}
$$

from

$$
\boxed{
\text{ECS-Discover}.
}
$$

That distinction is important.

### 26. Learning-theoretic interpretation

For a fixed finite grammar $\mathcal$ G, standard uniform-convergence reasoning gives

$\sup_{G\in\mathcal$ G}
$|\hat$ L(G)-L(G)|
$\lesssim$
$\sqrt{$
$\frac{$
$\log|\mathcal$ $G|+\log(1/\delta$)
}{
T
}
}.

Thus distinguishing candidates separated by population loss margin $\Delta$ requires roughly

$$
\boxed{
T
=
O\left(
\frac{
\log(|\mathcal G|/\delta)
}{
\Delta^2
}
\right).
}
$$

The important conceptual implication is that discovery complexity can depend on grammar complexity rather than ambient state dimension.

For adaptive grammar discovery, proposal and verification data should be separated.

This gives a statistical interpretation of the frozen experimental protocol:

$$
\boxed{
\text{proposal}
\rightarrow
\text{freeze}
\rightarrow
\text{independent evaluation}.
}
$$

### 27. Where theory predicts ECS should work

The strongest theoretical regime is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{localized}
+
\text{transported/transformed}
+
\text{repeated}
+
\text{reconstructible}.
}
$$

Examples include:

- localized deformation;
- impacts;
- vegetation interaction;
- traveling waves;
- particles;
- translated waveform components;
- sensor transients;
- moving anomalies;
- object pose;
- contact responses;
- repeated spatial mechanisms.

The common structure is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{low-dimensional cause}
\quad+\quad
\text{large ambient effect}.
}
$$

---

### 28. Networking interpretation

For synchronized interactive systems, the natural ECS representation is:

$$
\boxed{
e=
(
\text{mechanism ID},
\theta,
t_0,
\text{seed}
).
}
$$

Clients evaluate

$$
x(t)=G(e,t).
$$

Thus network cost can depend primarily on the number of causal parameters rather than the number of affected state variables.

This motivates the principle:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{replicate causes rather than outcomes whenever the resulting state is deterministically reconstructible.}
}
$$

The VR/graphics implementation branch is now testing this prediction.

---

### 29. What is established versus open

Established mathematically

We have rigorous elementary separation results showing that:

1. low-dimensional executable families can have arbitrarily large global linear approximation dimension;
2. the separation survives low coherence;
3. moving local residual frames can amplify the separation;
4. bounded local overlap preserves linear growth of required global rank;
5. random translations produce convolutional covariance with Fourier PCA modes;
6. translated Gaussian localization produces explicit diverging PCA rank while executable translation dimension remains fixed.

Established empirically

The frozen experiments show:

1. fallback models can and do win;
2. residual-guided generic grammar expansion can close large gaps;
3. tangent-space energy predicts when parameter refinement is appropriate;
4. generic executable mechanisms transferred across physical domains;
5. executable/hybrid representation won outright on one unseen physical dataset;
6. geometric equivalence analysis can prevent premature primitive proliferation;
7. heuristic predictions can be falsified without breaking the discovery procedure.

Not established

We have not shown that:

- ECS universally beats PCA;
- ECS beats general nonlinear autoencoders;
- arbitrary executable grammars can be discovered efficiently;
- the current grammar is optimal;
- the real-data results alone establish broad superiority;
- the current mathematical phenomena are themselves wholly novel.

Those claims should not be made.

---

### 30. Current mathematical frontier

The next major theory problem is not another separation theorem.

It is the inverse problem:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Given only samples, can we recognize that their large linear span is generated by a small executable transformation family?}
}
$$

Potential signals include:

- low local dimension but high global PCA dimension;
- systematic transformation relationships among PCA modes;
- coherent transport of local tangent spaces;
- cycle-consistent transformations between neighboring states;
- covariance symmetries;
- repeated residual directions under transformations.

A particularly promising route is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{PCA failure}
\rightarrow
\text{detect transformed repetition among modes}
\rightarrow
\text{infer candidate orbit}
\rightarrow
\text{test executable generator}.
}
$$

That would move ECS from testing candidate mechanisms toward genuinely discovering transformation grammars.

---

### 31. Current thesis

The strongest defensible thesis at this stage is:

> High-dimensional state variation can arise from low-dimensional executable mechanisms whose transformation orbits have large global linear complexity. ECS seeks compact representations of those mechanisms rather than their outcomes, combines them with small residual models when necessary, and uses residual geometry to determine when existing coordinates should be refined, when new directions are justified, and when apparently different candidate mechanisms are locally equivalent.

The theory identifies classes where executable representations have arbitrarily large advantages over global linear representations.

The experiments demonstrate that a frozen residual-driven discovery process can recover useful generic executable structure from real data and transfer it to previously unseen physical domains.

The implementation frontier is now to test whether the same predicted scaling appears in real-time graphics and networking.

# Editorial: reconciliation with this repository's record

Written 2026-10-06. The snapshot's empirical sections (19, 22, 23, 24
and the "established empirically" list in 29) cite a real-data
program on ECG200, ECGFiveDays, MoteStrain, Earthquakes and
SonyAIBORobotSurface1 (the v1 to v3 sequence, the tangent-energy
values 0.389, 0.510 and 0.004, the equivalence numbers 0.923, 0.995
and 0.097). **None of that is in this repository.** No code, data,
frozen configuration or results document for those experiments exists
here, and nothing in this record can confirm or deny those numbers.
Before a paper cites them, that program's artefacts (the frozen
runners, the datasets' provenance, the per-dataset results) have to be
attached to this repository or to a companion one, and the audit has
to cover them the way it covers the rounds below.

What this repository does hold, as of this date, is the record the
snapshot calls "the VR/graphics implementation branch", and it is
larger than the snapshot's section 28 suggests. In the snapshot's
terms:

- **ECS-Discover's core (sections 18 to 20) is implemented and
  frozen here** as the residual-guided procedure in `poc/rgre/core.py`:
  finite-difference tangents (secants from a declared on-state for a
  class that is off), projection of the residual onto each direction,
  the unexplained fraction `q_perp`, selection by the top direction,
  abstention at `q_perp > 0.5834` (the one fitted constant, calibrated
  in RGRE-1 and carried unchanged since), and a one-percent blacklist
  for spent repairs. Seven frozen rounds in three domains and
  fourteen in a fourth tested it (`docs/rgre-writeup.md`, the table in
  its section 3).
- **The tangential certificate (section 19) has a measured failure
  mode here.** `docs/linearisation-gap.md` measures, on 240 real-data
  steps and nine fire scenes, the fraction of a fitted repair that lies
  outside the tangent span the projection read: the projection
  captures the repair's full value where that gap is under 0.1 and
  half where it is over 0.25, and the fire plume's rise, the oracle's
  first step on five scenes of nine, had a gap of 0.98 and was
  invisible to the projection on every scene. On six real regression
  datasets the useful candidates were free-shape modules on 233 of
  240 steps, and there the projection captured half the oracle's
  value (RGRE-ML-2, outcome D). The snapshot's claim that first-order
  geometry "proposes which direction is missing before committing to a
  new syntax" holds where the repair is near-linear in the parameters
  and fails where its shape must be fitted; a growth procedure for
  real data must fit before it ranks. That caveat belongs next to
  section 20.
- **"Direction before syntax" (sections 21 and 22) has a nonlinear
  counterpart here**, the exclusivity table: for every ordered pair of
  classes, fit A to the teacher, then ask B to reproduce what A did;
  absorption(B|A) above a half means the two cannot be told apart by
  any selection rule (`poc/gait/dictionary_check.py`,
  `poc/gait3d/dictionary_check3d.py`). Built before G2 it predicted
  which impairments would be named, and the strongest form is
  algebraic: a class that is an exact combination of base parameters
  has a twin with identical output and can name nothing (G5, G6).
  G13 added that on a real clip the naming depends on the base's own
  asymmetry: the same damage on two sides of one body named
  differently.
- **The growth rule itself is the best-replicated result here.**
  Held-out acceptance (a repair is applied only if it lowers the mean
  error on two runs the fit never saw) with a per-consumer guard
  generalised on eight or nine damaged bodies of nine in every one of
  eleven 3D locomotion rounds, on a grammar base (G7 to G11) and on a
  clip base (G13, G14). It is the procedure's "frozen prediction then
  evaluate on untouched data" step (section 25) made quantitative.
- **The deployment frontier (sections 16 and 17) was measured, not
  assumed**: the cost-against-error envelope has a knee per consumer
  (26 to 48 ops per stalk for the eye, 20 for gameplay), measured
  milliseconds on hardware disagree with op counts by up to eight
  times (`docs/bendbench-results.md`), and the fallback won where it
  should (PCA-16 is not in this record; the generic fallbacks here are
  the baked field and the teacher itself).
- **Section 28's networking principle is bounded by a check here.**
  "Replicate causes rather than outcomes" holds for bandwidth and
  state, as the snapshot says, and it does not make local
  reconstruction beat a streamed render at low latency: the delayed
  truth's error is first order in the delay over the effect's time
  scale, and local reconstruction pays only above
  `L* = e_local * tau_eff`, 76 to 118 ms for grass, predicted within
  10 ms with no fitted constant (`docs/streaming-latency-check.md`).
  On a dedicated low-latency link the stream wins. The compute
  argument is unchanged; the latency argument has a threshold.
- **The locomotion arc adds a result the snapshot does not have**: the
  description a growth rule can name and the description a viewer
  accepts were different objects in that domain (four rounds of adding
  consumers never closed the eye's gap), the deployable description
  became the teacher's own clip with the grammar's classes as edits of
  it, and dropped parts are free while composed edits land within a
  quarter of a fresh fit (`docs/gait3d-runtime.md`, G13, G14).

So of the snapshot's seven "established empirically" items: 1
(fallbacks win) and 3 (tangent energy predicts refinement) hold here
with the linearisation-gap caveat; 2 (residual-guided expansion closes
gaps) holds in four domains at a sixth to a tenth of the search where
repairs are near-linear and fails where they are not; 4, 5 and 6 rest
on the ECG and MoteStrain program that is not in this record; 7
(heuristics falsified without breaking the procedure) has many
instances here (every round's predictions are scored, and G13's and
G14's were wrong in direction and right about the margins).

# Editorial: first-pass audit of the mathematics

> The full audit, with the numerical checks and the confirmed citations, is `docs/ecs-audit.md`; the table below is the summary it started from.


The snapshot asks for an audit of every theorem against the
approximation, manifold and transport literature before a paper is
built. This is the first pass, from memory of the literature, and
every citation below is to be verified against the source before it
is relied on; the classification is the point.

Reading: sections 2 to 15 are elementary or known results restated in
the ECS framing, and the paper should say so, with the citations
above once verified, and claim the framing and the pairing of the
separation with a discovery protocol rather than the inequalities.
That agrees with the snapshot's own "not established" list, which
already declines to claim novelty for the mathematical phenomena.
The candidate contributions that survive this pass are: the frozen
residual-guided discovery protocol with fallback and abstention, as a
procedure with a measured failure mode (the linearisation gap); the
exclusivity and equivalence tests as the nonlinear and first-order
forms of "direction before syntax"; the held-out growth rule's
replication across domains; the deployment knee measured on hardware;
and the real-data program once its artefacts are attached. The
inverse problem in section 30 should be framed against the
transformation-learning literature from the start.
